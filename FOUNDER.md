# The founder's story

I'm DeAli Dillard. I build Perseus.

For most of the last several years I watched the same thing happen over and over: someone brilliant, or someone just curious, would want to build something with AI — and the first thing they'd learn is that they can't. Not really. The intelligence lives somewhere else. It arrives through an API. It is priced by the token. It is rented.

You don't own it. You don't hold it. If the price changes, or the service changes, or the company decides you're not a customer anymore, your thing stops working. You built on sand you don't control.

I kept coming back to one question: **what if intelligence ran on the machine you already own?**

Not a smaller copy of someone else's model. Not a wrapper. A genuinely different arrangement — a dense, local mind that belongs to the person running it. Something you could put on the laptop already sitting on your desk and just… keep.

That question became Perseus.

## What I'm actually building

Perseus is a wave-dynamic runtime — its own engine, with its own language, Morpheus (`.morph`). The whole bet is density and coherence: a small number of coupled units, working in sync, adding up to something that holds together as a mind. Not scale for its own sake. Not a bigger bill. Density you can hold in your hand.

The reason it matters is simple. If the intelligence is local, then the person using it owns it. No meter. No dependency. No one able to switch it off from a building you'll never visit. That is a different relationship with the technology — and I think it's the one most people actually want.

## Where I am

I'm a solo founder. Aporia Nous Computing is a sole proprietorship out of Sioux Falls, South Dakota; I started it in August 2026. No headcount theater, no pretending to be bigger than I am. Just the work.

The runtime is real. I can show it running, measure it, and let you check the numbers yourself. The English the system produces is still rough — that's a readout I'm actively working on, not the engine. I'd rather be honest about that than dress it up.

I'm building this in the open because the point was never to keep it to myself. The point is that people should be able to run their own intelligence, and that starts with being able to see it.
